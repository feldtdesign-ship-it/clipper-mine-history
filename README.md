# Clipper Mine history

The Clipper (Lounsbury) Mine on Ester Dome, near Fairbanks, Alaska,
and the seismograph station that ran inside it from 1970 to 1972.

Every line below has a source. Full citations are in SOURCES.md.
Evidence grades: **Document** (a primary document says it) and **Reported** (a person or secondary source says it).
Our own inferences are kept in OPEN-QUESTIONS.md.

Abbreviations: DGGS (Alaska Division of Geological and Geophysical Surveys), OFR (Open-File Report),
USGS (US Geological Survey), ARDF (Alaska Resource Data File), DTIC (Defense Technical Information Center),
ARPA (Advanced Research Projects Agency), AFOSR (Air Force Office of Scientific Research),
NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration), UAF (University of Alaska Fairbanks).

## How we know the station was in the Clipper

1. The 1971 Lamont report names the station "Old Clipper Mine". (Document. DTIC AD0729918, report p. 1. Read it at https://archive.org/details/DTIC_AD0729918)
2. The 1982 state mine map labels a side drift "Lamont-Doherty Seismograph Station (Abandoned)". (Document. DGGS OFR 157, Plate 1.)
3. The report's own location map puts the station at Section 32, where the mine is. (Document. AD0729918, Figure I-2, report p. 4.)

## The mine

- The Clipper is on the south flank of Ester Dome, near the head of Eva Creek, in the SW quarter of sec. 32,
  T. 1 N., R. 2 W., Fairbanks Meridian. (Document. OFR 157 p. 1.)
- Development work began in 1927. (Document. OFR 157 p. 1.)
- By 1932 a 235 to 250 ft drift had been driven on a thin, steep vein. (Document. ARDF FB047, printed p. 101, citing Hill 1933 and Pilgrim.)
- The owner at that time, Lloyd Lounsbury, mined most of those workings by hand. (Reported. D. Wietchy, oral communication, 2000, cited in ARDF FB047, printed p. 101.)
- 19 tons of ore were milled in 1937 and 25 tons in 1938, at about 0.8 oz gold per ton. (Document. ARDF FB047, printed p. 102, citing Reed 1938.)
- The best years were 1937 to 1942: about 1,400 oz of gold, worth $50,000. (Reported. G.P. Lounsbury, in OFR 157 p. 3.)
- Lloyd Lounsbury, G.P. Lounsbury's father, ran the ore through a Blake jaw crusher and a 10 ton per day Straub ball mill at the mine. (Reported. G.P. Lounsbury, in OFR 157 p. 3.)
- In the late 1960s the mine and mill were open for guided tours. (Reported. G.P. Lounsbury, in OFR 157 p. 3.)

## The station, 1970 to 1972

- It was Columbia University's Lamont-Doherty station, paid for by ARPA (Advanced Research Projects Agency)
  through the Air Force Office of Scientific Research (AFOSR) for nuclear-test-detection research.
  (Document. AD0729918 cover and report form, PDF pp. 1 and 3; the program's 1973 report opens its results with
  "long-period discriminants between explosions and earthquakes", AD0760585 report p. 5.)
- A four-person Lamont crew installed it between 17 August and 26 September 1970: Merrill Conner, Shannon Cory,
  Tracy Johnson and Andrew J. Murphy. (Document. AD0729918, report p. 1.)
- The report names Lloyd Lounsbury as station manager, and Herbert Mann and George Lounsbury as station attendants. (Document. AD0729918, report p. 1.)
- The station sat in a side tunnel about 130 m from the entrance, under 20 m of rock, split into four rooms behind
  ship-type bulkhead doors. (Document. AD0729918, report p. 11.)
- In March 1971 the report said the mine "is presently being used as a tourist attraction". (Document. AD0729918, report p. 3.)
- A second contract, F44620-71-C-0082, ran from 15 March 1971 to 14 March 1972, for $295,000, covering the
  whole program of stations. (Document. DTIC AD0744130, PDF p. 5.)
- A two-man Lamont team closed the station in the last two weeks of April 1972 and shipped the equipment to NOAA
  at Albuquerque. The final recordings are dated 25 April 1972. (Document. DTIC AD0760585, report p. 5.)

## After the station

- In 1982 state geologists mapped about 800 ft of workings. G.P. Lounsbury was the owner. (Document. OFR 157 p. 1.)
- The USMS 2493 Grass Valley plat, claim of James G. and George H. Lounsbury, was recorded in 2001. (Document. Plat, recording number 50-2001-0164.)
- In July 2013 Clutch and Lorna Lounsbury hosted a car club picnic and mine tour. (Reported. Nash's News, Aug 2013, p. 9.)
- In June 2017 the Mining History Association toured the mine. (Reported. mininghistoryassociation.org/LodeMine.htm.)

## A known error

OFR 157 (p. 3) says the station went in "in the early 1960s". The Lamont reports date it to 1970.
See OPEN-QUESTIONS.md.
