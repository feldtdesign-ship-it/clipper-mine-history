# Open questions

Gaps, conflicts and our own inferences.
Anything here is not settled. Nothing here goes in the timeline as fact.
Lines marked "Our reading" are our own inference.

Abbreviations: DGGS (Alaska Division of Geological and Geophysical Surveys),
OFR (Open-File Report), ARDF (Alaska Resource Data File), USGS (US Geological
Survey), DTIC (Defense Technical Information Center), FNSB (Fairbanks North
Star Borough), LiDAR (laser scanning), FBK (the Fairbanks station code).

## Known errors and conflicts in the sources

### OFR 157 dates the station to "the early 1960s"
- OFR 157 p. 3 says Lamont-Doherty installed the station "in the early 1960s".
- The Lamont 1971 report (DTIC AD0729918, report p. 1) dates the installation 17 Aug to 26 Sep 1970.
- The two Lamont annual reports (AD0744130, AD0760585) put operation in 1971 and the close in April 1972.
- Treated as a known error in OFR 157. OFR 157 says its history came from the owner, G.P. Lounsbury (p. 1).
- Kept out of the timeline.

### The printed station position is about 3.6 km off
- The Lamont 1971 report prints 64 53 58 N, 148 00 20 W, 330 m (report p. 3, and the abstract on PDF p. 3).
- That point falls about 3.6 km north of the mine.
- The same report's Figure I-2 (report p. 4, PDF p. 11) puts the "New Vault" arrow at Section 32, where the mine is.
- The report itself says "Old Clipper Mine" (report p. 1) and "Old Clipper Seismic Station" (Plate 7).
- New, Nov 2026: the USMS 2493 plat prints a position at Corner No. 1 of the Grass Valley claim that reads about
  64 51 5x N, 148 00 23.70 W. The seconds of latitude are blurry in our scan.
- Our reading: "53" in the Lamont report is likely a typo for "51". No document prints 64 51 58 for the station.
  The plat corner position is close to that reading but is a claim corner, not the station.

### The 1970 site sheet disagrees with the 1971 report
- Lamont's site sheet of 28 Aug 1970 (DTIC AD0730950, report p. 7) and the 1971 installation report
  (AD0729918) differ:
  - Rock over the station: 130 m on the sheet, 20 m in the 1971 report (report p. 11).
  - Longitude: 148.07 W on the sheet, 148 00 20 W (148.006) in 1971. 148.07 W would be about 3 km west of the mine.
  - Elevation: about 305 m on the sheet, 330 m in 1971.
  - Latitude on the sheet reads 64.?7 N; the second decimal is blurry (8 or 3).
- Our reading: the sheet was written during the install, before final survey. 64.87 N would match the mine
  (about 64.866 N) and supports the "53 is a typo for 51" reading above. Unproven.

### The abstract says 15 km from the College vault
- The Lamont 1971 abstract (PDF p. 3) says the tunnel is about 15 km from the WWSSN (World-Wide Standardized
  Seismograph Network) station COL vault. Measured off Figure I-2 it is closer to 11 km.
- Our reading: 15 km may be a road distance. Unproven.

### Portal elevation
- OFR 157 p. 1: the mine is "at the 1100 ft elevation".
- ARDF FB047 printed p. 100: "an elevation of about 1,100 feet".
- Lamont 1971 report p. 3: station elevation 330 m (1,083 ft), printed with the position that falls 3.6 km away.
- FNSB 2017 LiDAR ground at our modeled portal: about 293 m (961 ft). The modeled portal is traced, not surveyed.
- Unresolved.

### Nash's News claims (club lore)
- The Aug 2013 newsletter (p. 9) calls the Clipper "the last patented mine in Alaska". No other source supports it.
- It calls the room shown on the tour the mine's "once top secret mission". No document calls the station secret.
  The Lamont reports were approved for public release.
- Both kept as club lore. Neither goes in the timeline as fact.

### The plat may be dated 1988
- The USMS 2493 plat scan appears to read "Surveyed June 4, 1988 to June 1?, 1988" and a certification date of
  "?? December 1988" (lower right). The scan is too blurry to be sure.
- The timeline dates the plat only as recorded in 2001.
- To settle it: read the original at the recorder's office, or get a clean copy from the BLM (Bureau of Land
  Management).

### Film length
- The UAF archive record calls AAF-1869 a 55-second clip. Our copy runs about 74.5 seconds.
- Our reading: the extra time is archive leader or a slate. Not checked.

### The Fairbanks abstract says three bulkhead doors; the report body says two
- The abstract of the 1971 Fairbanks report repeats a sentence used in all the installation reports: "a chamber
  sealed from the environment by three ship-type bulkhead doors."
- The body of the same report says "Only two bulkhead doors (#5100) were installed." The timeline uses two.
- The three sister stations each report three doors.

### Eilat: "Mine" on the 1970 site sheet, a purpose-built tunnel in the 1971 report
- The 1970 site sheet (DTIC AD0730950, PDF p. 10) gives the Eilat station type as "Mine".
- The 1971 Eilat report (DTIC AD0729899, PDF p. 14) says the tunnel "was constructed as a geophysical observatory
  in 1968". The timeline follows the 1971 report.

### The 1972 test month is printed two ways
- AD0751960 (report p. 7) prints "20 March - 20 February, 1972". AD0778950 (report p. 6) prints "20 February
  1972 through 19 March 1972". The timeline uses the second.
- The 1973 count rests on a preliminary list of 840 events. The 1974 annual report (DTIC AD0784837, report p. 6)
  says the final list had 996 entries and does not restate the sentence about five stations.

## Our inferences, not proven

### Shaft
- OFR 157 p. 1 and ARDF FB047 printed p. 102 say there are older caved workings about 60 vertical ft above the portal.
- The USMS 2493 plat shows a shaft. In our model it sits about 56 ft above the modeled portal.
- Our reading: likely the same old workings. Unproven.

### The Nash's News room
- Our reading: the room shown to the 2013 tour is the old station room. The newsletter does not say so.

### "M. Connor" at Ogdensburg in 1975
- Document: a 1975 Lamont report names "M. Connor" as the one technician at the Ogdensburg observatory.
- Document: the 1971 Fairbanks and Eilat reports name crew member "Merrill Conner". The 1971 instrumentation
  report (DTIC AD0729902, report p. 1) lists a contributor "Merrill Connor".
- Our reading: likely the same man. No document gives the 1975 technician a first name. Unproven.

## Open questions

### Why did the Fairbanks station close after two years?
- The reports give the date and the method (April 1972, a two-man team, equipment shipped to Albuquerque).
- None of the twelve Lamont reports read for the 25 Nov 2026 update gives a reason.
- Five more reports on the two contracts have been asked of the Library of Congress: DTIC AD735765, AD765377,
  AD746798, AD752001 and ADA024627.

### Toledo
- Planned as the fifth station; delayed to spring 1971. No installation report for it has been found.
- Who installed it, and when did it start recording?

### Before 1970: did anything happen at the Clipper?
- OFR 157 says "early 1960s" (see above).
- Every document found so far puts the first instruments in the mine at 17 Aug 1970.
- What the documents do show near the mine before 1970 (see the "Around the mine" timeline rows): the College
  seismograph from 1957, Lamont's College station from 10 Dec 1963, the University's Ester Dome observatory from
  1962 to 1963, and Lamont's own mine station in New Jersey from early 1960.
- Our reading: an early date for the station may come from memory of this wider work around the mine. Unproven.
- Who first proposed the Clipper to Lamont? Not known. Jack Townshend was Lamont's contact in Aug 1970.
  Jurgen Kienle was at Lamont from 1968 to 1970. No document links either man to choosing the mine.
- Where were the University's 1963 to 1964 site noise studies, run with Lamont's loaned equipment? Not stated.


### People
- Was Lloyd Lounsbury, station manager in 1970 (Lamont 1971, report p. 1), the same Lloyd who mined the Clipper
  by hand by 1932 (ARDF FB047, citing D. Wietchy, 2000)?
  - OFR 157 p. 3 calls Lloyd Lounsbury the father of G.P. Lounsbury.
  - Nash's News p. 9 says the mine "was worked by Jim and George Lounsbury's father".
  - Still open: no single document ties the 1930s Lloyd to the 1970 Lloyd.
- Is G.P. Lounsbury (OFR 157) George Lounsbury? The plat names "George H. Lounsbury". The middle initials differ
  (P and H). Unresolved.
- Who is D. Wietchy, cited in the ARDF?
- Who shot the George Lounsbury Collection film (AAF-1869)? The archive record does not say.

### Mill
- OFR 157 p. 3: a Blake jaw crusher and a 10 ton per day Straub ball mill, not a stamp mill.
- Where did they stand? The plat shows a cabin and an outbuilding but no mill.
