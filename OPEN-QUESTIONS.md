# Open questions

Gaps, conflicts and our own inferences.
Anything here is not settled. Nothing here goes in the timeline as fact.
Lines marked "Our reading" are our own inference.

Abbreviations: DGGS (Alaska Division of Geological and Geophysical Surveys),
OFR (Open-File Report), ARDF (Alaska Resource Data File), USGS (US Geological
Survey), DTIC (Defense Technical Information Center), FNSB (Fairbanks North
Star Borough), LiDAR (laser scanning), FBK (the Fairbanks station code).

## Known errors and conflicts in the sources

### OFR 157 dates the station to "the early 1960s"
- OFR 157 p. 3 says Lamont-Doherty installed the station "in the early 1960s".
- The Lamont 1971 report (DTIC AD0729918, report p. 1) dates the installation 17 Aug to 26 Sep 1970.
- The two Lamont annual reports (AD0744130, AD0760585) put operation in 1971 and the close in April 1972.
- Treated as a known error in OFR 157. OFR 157 says its history came from the owner, G.P. Lounsbury (p. 1).
- Kept out of the timeline.

### The printed station position is about 3.6 km off
- The Lamont 1971 report prints 64 53 58 N, 148 00 20 W, 330 m (report p. 3, and the abstract on PDF p. 3).
- That point falls about 3.6 km north of the mine.
- The same report's Figure I-2 (report p. 4, PDF p. 11) puts the "New Vault" arrow at Section 32, where the mine is.
- The report itself says "Old Clipper Mine" (report p. 1) and "Old Clipper Seismic Station" (Plate 7).
- New, Nov 2026: the USMS 2493 plat prints a position at Corner No. 1 of the Grass Valley claim that reads about
  64 51 5x N, 148 00 23.70 W. The seconds of latitude are blurry in our scan.
- Our reading: "53" in the Lamont report is likely a typo for "51". No document prints 64 51 58 for the station.
  The plat corner position is close to that reading but is a claim corner, not the station.

### The 1970 site sheet disagrees with the 1971 report
- Lamont's site sheet of 28 Aug 1970 (DTIC AD0730950, report p. 7) and the 1971 installation report
  (AD0729918) differ:
  - Rock over the station: 130 m on the sheet, 20 m in the 1971 report (report p. 11).
  - Longitude: 148.07 W on the sheet, 148 00 20 W (148.006) in 1971. 148.07 W would be about 3 km west of the mine.
  - Elevation: about 305 m on the sheet, 330 m in 1971.
  - Latitude on the sheet reads 64.?7 N; the second decimal is blurry (8 or 3).
- Our reading: the sheet was written during the install, before final survey. 64.87 N would match the mine
  (about 64.866 N) and supports the "53 is a typo for 51" reading above. Unproven.
- Added 28 Nov 2026, both pages checked by eye. The 1971 report says the station is "approximately 130 m from
  the entrance and beneath 20 m of overburden" (report p. 11). Our reading: the two rock figures may not be a
  true conflict. The 1970 sheet may have put the distance into the hill, 130 m, where the depth of rock
  belonged. Unproven. No document says which is right, but the 1971 report was written after the station
  was built.

### The abstract says 15 km from the College vault
- The Lamont 1971 abstract (PDF p. 3) says the tunnel is about 15 km from the WWSSN (World-Wide Standardized
  Seismograph Network) station COL vault. Measured off Figure I-2 it is closer to 11 km.
- Our reading: 15 km may be a road distance. Unproven.

### Portal elevation
- OFR 157 p. 1: the mine is "at the 1100 ft elevation".
- ARDF FB047 printed p. 100: "an elevation of about 1,100 feet".
- Lamont 1971 report p. 3: station elevation 330 m (1,083 ft), printed with the position that falls 3.6 km away.
- FNSB 2017 LiDAR ground at our modeled portal: about 293 m (961 ft). The modeled portal is traced, not surveyed.
- Unresolved.

### Nash's News claims (club lore)
- The Aug 2013 newsletter (p. 9) calls the Clipper "the last patented mine in Alaska". No other source supports it.
- It calls the room shown on the tour the mine's "once top secret mission". No document calls the station secret.
  The Lamont reports were approved for public release.
- Both kept as club lore. Neither goes in the timeline as fact.

### The plat is dated 1988, not 2001
- Read again by eye on 28 Nov 2026, enlarged. The handwritten certification reads "2? December 1988". The day
  is 20 or 26. The year is clear. The timeline now dates the plat December 1988.
- The line "Surveyed June 4, 19?? to June ??, 19??" is still not readable in our scan. It looks like 1988.
- The number 50-2001-0164 stamped on the left margin has no label on the sheet. Earlier versions of this repo
  called it a recording number. It matches the serial of the federal patent dated 21 Dec 2000.
- The right margin is stamped BK 01244 PG 0923, a recorder's book and page. So our scan is a recorded copy.
  The sheet itself carries no recording date.
- To settle the rest: get a clean copy of the plat and the patent from the BLM (Bureau of Land Management) or
  the recorder's office.

### Lloyd Lounsbury: owner or lessee in the 1930s
- Reported: the ARDF, citing D. Wietchy in 2000, calls Lloyd Lounsbury "the owner at that time" of the
  workings driven by 1932 (ARDF FB047, printed pp. 101 and 102).
- Document: Reed 1938 (printed pp. 8 to 9) names seven owners of the Clipper Mine and says "It is under lease
  to Lloyd P. Lounsbury." Lounsbury is not one of the seven.
- Document: the territorial report for 1932 (printed p. 8) has John Mikely doing the annual assessment work on
  the Clipper vein. It does not name Lounsbury.
- Document: by 28 Aug 1970, Lamont's site sheet says "Owned by: Mr. Lloyd Loundsbury" (DTIC AD0730950).
- Both versions are kept in the timeline. The period documents are closer to the events than the 2000 account.
- Still open: when and how did the Lounsburys go from lease to ownership?

### Production: two figures that have never been reconciled
- Document: Reed 1938 (printed p. 9): about 19 tons in 1937 and about 25 tons in 1938, with an average value of
  $28 to $30 a ton. Reed gives dollars, not ounces. The "0.8 oz per ton" in OFR 157 and the ARDF is a later
  restatement.
- Reported: G.P. Lounsbury told the state geologists in 1982 that 1937 to 1942 gave about 1,400 oz worth
  $50,000 (OFR 157 p. 3). OFR 157 says its stope volumes and assays support that level.
- Our reading: Reed's two years come to roughly 36 oz. The rest would have to come from 1939 to 1942, for which
  no mill, mint or smelter record has been found. The two figures cover different years, so they do not
  contradict each other, but nothing on file confirms the larger one.

### Film length
- The UAF archive record calls AAF-1869 a 55-second clip. Our copy runs about 74.5 seconds.
- Our reading: the extra time is archive leader or a slate. Not checked.

### The Fairbanks abstract says three bulkhead doors; the report body says two
- The abstract of the 1971 Fairbanks report repeats a sentence used in all the installation reports: "a chamber
  sealed from the environment by three ship-type bulkhead doors."
- The body of the same report says "Only two bulkhead doors (#5100) were installed." The timeline uses two.
- The three sister stations each report three doors.
- Added 28 Nov 2026, checked by eye: a third count is in print. The 1971 instrumentation report says the
  seismometer vaults at Fairbanks, Eilat and Toledo are "at the end of a tunnel enclosed by one bulkhead door"
  (DTIC AD0729902, report p. 15, PDF p. 19). That sentence is about the seismometer vault alone.
- Our reading: one door closed the seismometer vault and a second stood elsewhere in the station, which makes
  the two that the Fairbanks report says were installed. The Fairbanks report says the crew poured "a single,
  hollow concrete plug" in place of two walls, with the hollow part used as the phototube amplifier room
  (report p. 11). Where each of the two doors sat in that plug is not yet worked out. Unproven.

### Eilat: "Mine" on the 1970 site sheet, a purpose-built tunnel in the 1971 report
- The 1970 site sheet (DTIC AD0730950, PDF p. 10) gives the Eilat station type as "Mine".
- The 1971 Eilat report (DTIC AD0729899, PDF p. 14) says the tunnel "was constructed as a geophysical observatory
  in 1968". The timeline follows the 1971 report.

### The 1972 test month is printed two ways
- AD0751960 (report p. 7) prints "20 March - 20 February, 1972". AD0778950 (report p. 6) prints "20 February
  1972 through 19 March 1972". The timeline uses the second.
- The 1973 count rests on a preliminary list of 840 events. The 1974 annual report (DTIC AD0784837, report p. 6)
  says the final list had 996 entries and does not restate the sentence about five stations.

## Our inferences, not proven

### Shaft
- OFR 157 p. 1 and ARDF FB047 printed p. 102 say there are older caved workings about 60 vertical ft above the portal.
- The USMS 2493 plat shows a shaft. In our model it sits about 56 ft above the modeled portal.
- Our reading: likely the same old workings. Unproven.

### The Nash's News room
- Our reading: the room shown to the 2013 tour is the old station room. The newsletter does not say so.

### "M. Connor" at Ogdensburg in 1975
- Document: a 1975 Lamont report names "M. Connor" as the one technician at the Ogdensburg observatory.
- Document: the 1971 Fairbanks and Eilat reports name crew member "Merrill Conner". The 1971 instrumentation
  report (DTIC AD0729902, report p. 1) lists a contributor "Merrill Connor".
- Our reading: likely the same man. No document gives the 1975 technician a first name. Unproven.

### "Cory, S.E." and the Toledo report
- Document: the 1972 annual report cites "Cory, S.E., High-gain, long-period seismograph station installation
  report, Toledo, Spain", Lamont-Doherty, April 1972 (DTIC AD0744130, report p. 17).
- Document: the 1971 Fairbanks report names crew member Shannon Cory (DTIC AD0729918, report p. 1).
- Our reading: likely the same person. No document read gives the full first name of S.E. Cory. Unproven.

### Was the mine on unpatented federal land in 1970?
- Document: the plat was certified in December 1988 as the "claim of" James G. and George H. Lounsbury. A
  federal patent followed on 21 Dec 2000.
- Document: in 1932 "annual assessment work" was being done on the Clipper vein, which is the yearly work
  needed to hold an unpatented claim.
- Our reading: the ground was probably still an unpatented mining claim on federal land when the station
  was built. No 1970 document says so. Nor does any document tie the name Grass Valley to the 1930s Clipper
  claim. Unproven.

### Does the 1973 depth finding explain the Fairbanks horizontal noise?
- Document: in 1971 Lamont wrote that horizontal noise at Fairbanks "can undergo order of magnitude variations
  in amplitude", that it "does not appear on the vertical component", and that "A special investigation into
  this noise is under way" (DTIC AD0729897, report p. 15).
- Document: in 1973 Lamont wrote that its Ogdensburg experiment "indicates that horizontal-component
  seismometers should be placed at depths of 150 to 200m below the surface at a hard rock site" (DTIC
  AD0778950, report p. 2).
- Document: the Fairbanks station sat beneath 20 m of overburden, and "the rock between the console room and
  the seismometer chamber is badly fractured" (DTIC AD0729918, report p. 11).
- Our reading: Fairbanks was far shallower than the depth Lamont later called for, and that may be why its
  horizontals were noisy and why it was the station closed. Unproven. No document gives a reason for the
  closure. In 1971 Lamont also wrote that noise "is obviously not dependent solely upon depth of overburden"
  (AD0729897, report p. 14), and the 1970 site sheet lists Charters Towers as "Mine, 33 meters overburden"
  (DTIC AD0730950, PDF p. 10). That sheet's rock figure for Fairbanks is in doubt (see above), so its figure for
  Charters Towers should be checked against the Charters Towers installation report before it is relied on.

## Open questions

### Why did the Fairbanks station close after two years?
- The reports give the date and the method (April 1972, a two-man team, equipment shipped to Albuquerque).
- None of the twelve Lamont reports read for the 25 Nov 2026 update gives a reason.
- Five more reports on the two contracts have been asked of the Library of Congress: DTIC AD735765, AD765377,
  AD746798, AD752001 and ADA024627.
- Added 28 Nov 2026: one possible reason, our reading only, is under "Does the 1973 depth finding explain the
  Fairbanks horizontal noise?" above.
- The 1972 annual report (DTIC AD0744130, report p. 5), written after 15 Mar 1972, describes plans to find
  digital technicians "in Spain, Israel, and Thailand" and to ship a new digital unit to Australia. It gives no
  such plan for Fairbanks. Our reading: the decision to close Fairbanks had been made by then. Unproven.

### Toledo
- Planned as the fifth station; delayed to spring 1971.
- Answered in part, 28 Nov 2026. The 1972 annual report (DTIC AD0744130, report p. 6) says the station was
  installed from 15 Jul to 17 Aug 1971, and cites an installation report by "Cory, S.E.", April 1972 (report
  p. 17). Both pages checked by eye. It is now a timeline row.
- Still open: no copy of that Toledo installation report has been found. Who else was on the crew?

### Before 1970: did anything happen at the Clipper?
- OFR 157 says "early 1960s" (see above).
- Every document found so far puts the first instruments in the mine at 17 Aug 1970.
- What the documents do show near the mine before 1970 (see the "Around the mine" timeline rows): the College
  seismograph from 1957, Lamont's College station from 10 Dec 1963, the University's Ester Dome observatory from
  1962 to 1963, and Lamont's own mine station in New Jersey from early 1960.
- Our reading: an early date for the station may come from memory of this wider work around the mine. Unproven.
- Who first proposed the Clipper to Lamont? Not known. Jack Townshend was Lamont's contact in Aug 1970.
  Jurgen Kienle was at Lamont from 1968 to 1970. No document links either man to choosing the mine.
- Where were the University's 1963 to 1964 site noise studies, run with Lamont's loaned equipment? Not stated.
